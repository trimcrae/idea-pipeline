## What the probe tests
One question: **will a would-be tiny food seller give an email for a one-state
"what can I legally sell and what permit do I need" answer?** Selling out of
compliance = shutdown/fines, and the laws are notoriously confusing — new
sellers search them constantly.

## Pick ONE state for v1
Start with one state (placeholder **[State]**) — ideally one with an active
cottage-food scene and a confusing rulebook.

## Pass bar (SET — do not move)
**≥10 self-serve sign-ups** from search-intent traffic and/or one community post,
~one week. Below that = no signal → reshape or kill, log, move.

## Asset A — landing page (one button; no product behind it)
> **Headline:** Want to sell your [baked goods] in [State]? See exactly what's
> legal and which permit you need — before you start.
>
> **Sub:** Pick your product and county. Get what cottage-food / mobile-vendor
> law allows, the permit and inspection steps, and the limits (revenue caps,
> labeling) — in plain language, not health-department PDF-speak.
>
> **The one thing it does:** one current, structured answer instead of scattered
> Facebook-group threads.
>
> **CTA:** Check what I can sell · [email field]
>
> **Honest footer:** Early. Starting with [State] to confirm it's wanted before expanding.

## Asset C — tiny ad (recommended)
Target "[State] cottage food law / sell baked goods from home [State] permit"
intent — high, constant search volume from new sellers.

## Honest note / catch
Laws change and vary by county (`maint:manual`); blogs/FB groups cover this
loosely (`crowd:med`). Wedge = a current, structured, per-state answer. Tiny-
business payer = low price point, so keep build and price small.
